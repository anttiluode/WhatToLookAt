Gate 0 receipt. With an oracle relation graph, actively taking one sample from each of four components reconstructs this deliberately simple world from 4/256 pixels = 1.5625%. With random sampling, 16 measurements were the first tested budget to reach the 40 dB criterion on at least 90% of 48 masks. The spatial and shuffled controls did not reach it even at 128 measurements.
Why this is not victory yet. Gate 0 gives the system the answer-shaped relation graph. Gate 1 must earn that graph from past common-fate evidence and then use it on a future frame it has not seen. The shuffled-relation attacker exists precisely so “a graph” is not enough; it must be the right relation.

The central score for the project is B(Q): the minimum physical measurement cost needed to maintain quality Q. The aim is eventually to make B(Q) dynamic—low in a stable world, high during surprise, then low again after relearning.

Gate 1 — history earns the graph. Sixteen tracked parts carry eight-step motion histories. The four hidden objects use the same velocity vocabulary in different temporal orders. Ordered common fate clusters perfectly across the 48-world reference (median ARI 1.000); independently shuffling time drops median ARI to 0.000.
Then spend the history. After the parts are randomly rearranged, the learned relation reaches the 40 dB / 90% criterion at 12/256 random pixels, exactly matching the oracle relation. Time-shuffled history, absolute-coordinate memory, and the spatial lattice do not reach the criterion even at 128/256. Active learned sampling uses 4/256 pixels with 100% success.
Gate 2 — uncertainty buys observation. Wrong correspondences make blind four-measurement trust collapse to 0% success. A learned local confidence threshold instead grows the average budget from 4.00 → 5.99 → 7.95 → 9.95 measurements as 0 → 2 → 4 → 6 matches become wrong, while held-out success stays 100% → 96.25% → 95% → 95%.
Not just “measure more.” Global caution is perfect but always costs 16 measurements. Shuffling the same confidence values keeps almost the same adaptive budget but drops success to 1.25%, 0%, and 1.25% in the ambiguous worlds. The extra observations have to be spent where the relation is actually uncertain.
Gate 3 — the confidence signal is now an image measurement. Every patch pays for an 8-scalar 2×4 guide thumbnail; an expensive 8×8 RGB patch costs 192 scalars. Validation selects best-vs-second-best template margin (0.9759 balanced accuracy) over match error and cycle consistency. The guide cost is included in every adaptive budget.
The budget still localizes. With 4 ambiguous guide patches (3.35 wrong matches on average), blind trust succeeds 0% at 29.17% of a full scan. Image-confidence adaptive sensing succeeds 96.25% at 55.18%. Shuffling the same confidence values costs 54.79% but succeeds only 5%. At 6 ambiguous guides, adaptive sensing still succeeds 96.25% at 67.37% of full sensing.
Gate 4 — a relation is now a falsifiable compression hypothesis. One expensive anchor is read per relation. An 8-scalar current guide projection tests whether another member still matches that relation closely enough to inherit the anchor. Large residuals buy their own high-resolution read.
Matched-cost attacker. With 4 large innovations, fixed one-per-relation sensing collapses to 0% success at 29.17% of a full scan. Residual-adaptive sensing is 100% at 54.24%. Buying exactly the same number of extra expensive patches at random is still 0%. With 8 innovations, adaptive remains 100% at 78.62%, essentially the 78.54% oracle cost.
Gate 5 — sparse measurements have a measurable price. Relation failures now alter only 8/192 coordinates. Rows touching 1 coordinate never reach the 95% end-to-end validation target even with 64 guide rows; support 2 reaches only 92.5%. The missing coordinates are genuinely unobserved.
A useful middle regime appears. The selected support-64 guide needs 4 rows: 64 guide scalars and 4096 total coordinate touches. The selected dense support-192 guide also needs 4 rows and 64 guide scalars, but 12288 touches. On held-out worlds the support-64 guide stays at 98.75–100% success across 0–8 sparse innovations. Same observation count, about one-third the mixing work.
Gate 6 — design the sparse support before observation. In the one-coordinate innovation attacker, three 64-wide designed rows deliberately cover all 192 signal coordinates. That is enough for 100% held-out success across 0/2/4/6/8 innovation patches.
Pruning is not the same operation. Post-hoc top-weight pruning needs 12 sparse rows to reach the same held-out frontier: 12,288 guide coordinate touches versus 3,072 for coverage-designed sensing. Dense sensing reaches validation with two rows but pays 6,144 touches and falls to 93.33% at the six-innovation held-out level.
Gate 7 — full coordinate coverage still fails. The three-row designed sensor touches 192/192 coordinates and is perfect for one-coordinate innovations. With total innovation energy held fixed, its worst held-out success collapses to 44.17% at support 2 and 63.33% at support 4.
The missing object is measurement diversity. Twelve overlapping sparse rows stay at 100% for the post-hoc sensor and reach 99.17–100% for the random sparse sensor once support is at least 2. Multiple signed changes can cancel inside a coverage-only row, so “touch every coordinate” is weaker than “give every sparse possibility a distinguishable signature.”
